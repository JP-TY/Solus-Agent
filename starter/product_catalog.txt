## Philippine Net-Metering Framework — ERC Resolution No. 06 Series of 2019 (as amended)

### Eligibility
- End-users of distribution utilities (Meralco, VECO, Davao Light, and other DUs and ECs)
- Renewable system size up to 100 kWp per premises
- Solar PV is the dominant qualified technology

### Crediting rule — avoided generation cost vs retail rate
- Self-consumed solar offsets the full retail rate (generation + transmission + distribution + surcharges, e.g. approx PHP 11-13/kWh for Meralco residential)
- Exported surplus is credited at the DU's avoided generation cost (blended generation charge, e.g. approx PHP 5-7/kWh), NOT the full retail rate
- Credits appear as peso deductions on the monthly DU bill; net billing monthly

### Interconnection process
1. Ocular site survey and load assessment by installer
2. Net-metering application to DU with single-line diagram, inverter certs, PRC-licensed engineer sign-off
3. DU technical evaluation and meter socket inspection
4. Bi-directional meter installation and Certificate of Compliance
5. Permission to operate and first net-billed cycle

### Distribution utilities covered
- Meralco (Metro Manila, Cavite, Laguna, Bulacan, Rizal): online net-metering portal, 15-30 day evaluation typical
- VECO (Cebu): VECO Net-Metering desk, requires ERC Certificate of Compliance flow
- Davao Light (Davao City, Panabo): Davao Light renewables desk, similar documentary steps

## Solar Sizing Reference — Tropical Philippines

### Peak sun hours (PSH)
- National average: 4.5-5.0 PSH daily
- Use 4.5 PSH for conservative Luzon/Visayas/Mindanao sizing, 5.0 PSH for high-irradiance summer months
- System derate factor: 0.75-0.80 (soiling, heat, inverter losses, orientation)

### Sizing formula
- Daily kWh need = monthly kWh / 30
- Required kWp = daily kWh / (PSH x derate)
- Example: 300 kWh/month = 10 kWh/day; 10 / (4.5 x 0.78) = 2.85 kWp, round to 3.0-3.3 kWp (6 x 550W panels)

### Roof and typhoon guidance
- GI sheet: rail + L-foot + butyl sealing, extra purlin screws within 1m of eaves and ridges
- Concrete slab: ballasted or chemically anchored rails, waterproof membrane protection
- Orientation: south-facing ideal; east-west split acceptable with 5-8% yield penalty
- NSCP wind compliance: anodized aluminum rails, stainless fasteners, typhoon clips, design to 280+ km/h gusts in eastern seaboard zones

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How fast is payback in the Philippines?
At Meralco residential retail approx PHP 12/kWh and avoided export cost approx PHP 6/kWh, with 60% self-consumption and 40% export, a grid-tie system typically pays back in 4-6 years; hybrid with battery in 6-8 years including backup value.

### Does solar work during brownouts?
Grid-tie string inverters shut down during outages for lineman safety. Only hybrid inverters with batteries provide backup; critical-loads subpanel required.

### What roof information does Solus need?
Roof material (GI sheet or concrete slab), orientation, shading from trees or buildings, monthly bill in PHP or kWh, distribution utility (Meralco, VECO, Davao Light), and battery backup priority.

### What are Solus service tiers?
Silver: standard survey and install scheduling. Gold: 10% promo on accessories and priority scheduling. Platinum: 15% promo, same-day survey slots in Metro Manila and Cebu, priority net-metering liaison.
